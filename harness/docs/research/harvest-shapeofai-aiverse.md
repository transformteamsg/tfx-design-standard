### Dark Matter — full detail

This is the group the brief flagged as disproportionately valuable, so it is recorded in full rather than as a one-liner.

**Group definition, verbatim:**
> "Potentially nefarious, but certainly ambiguous patterns that impact user trust with questionable user value"

**Important finding:** as of this harvest the Dark Matter group contains **exactly one published pattern — Rating**. The group index page (`/pattern-types/dark-matter`) renders a single card. This is not a fetch failure: the page loaded fully (HTTP 200, complete markup, footer present) and lists one item. There is no larger hidden Dark Matter roster reachable at a public URL; the site's own `sitemap.xml` contains only the one Dark Matter member (`/patterns/rating`).

**Dark Matter #1 — Rating.** Group card rule, verbatim: *"Signal expectation gaps or errors in the model – but is that clear to the user?"*

The detail page gives the reasoning behind classifying it as harmful/ambiguous. Verbatim excerpts:

> "Giving users the ability to rate their interactions has become a table stakes pattern in service and conversational experiences (think chat support, or Uber)."

> "On its face this pattern is rather tame and familiar. Its potential risk factor may be buried and hidden to the user. What happens after they rate their experience?"

> "In scenario A, the user knows they have been interacting with the model. A thumbs-up or down signals to prompt engineers whether the design of the model itself is effective. This could be especially helpful for proprietary internal models or secure models trained on sensitive data."

> "In scenario B, the user doesn't know if they are interacting with a human or a model. OR they don't know what experiments the company is running to potential replace human engagement with digital engagement. Even an average person could feel put off by the ethical implications from that lack of transparency."

> "The effects on this may be innocuous, but with unknown actors in the space, and a lack of transparency into training data, asking for this type of input from the user without providing a direct benefit can degrade trust."

> "What we should expect to see, or at least hope to see, is more information about what happens based on the user's rating, and transparency to the user about whether they are rating the response to their request, or the model as a whole."

The extractable "do not do this" rule, in the source's own terms: do not collect a rating without (a) disclosing whether the user is rating a single response or the model as a whole, and (b) providing the user a direct benefit in return. Note the source's stance is *ambiguous / handle-with-care*, not an outright prohibition.

### AIverse gating — what is and is not publicly reachable

- **Publicly reachable in full:** the `/patterns` index. It publishes every pattern name, its phase, and a one-line rule for all 40. That is the entire rule column above, so the harvest is complete at rule level despite the paywall.
- **Partially gated:** the 23 reachable detail pages. Each renders the pattern name, phase, sub-category, restated one-line rule, a "User benefits" bullet list, and then the *first paragraph only* of the "Why does X matter?" essay before it is truncated mid-sentence and replaced by an upgrade wall reading "Unlock this pattern instantly with PRO."
- **Gated content not harvested:** on every detail page the "Key takeaways" numbered list, "Editor's notes", "Future play", and the example library are behind PRO. Section headings render; bodies do not.
- **Free vs PRO badging:** 6 patterns are badged `Free` (Suggested prompts, Voice input, Prompting assistance, Streaming + Realtime, Preview, Review actions, Feedback — 7 by count of badges on the index); the rest carry `PRO Exclusive`. Badging did not change what was reachable — the truncation-plus-wall structure was identical on Free and PRO pages.
- **No detail page at all:** the 11 NEW patterns plus Disclaimer and Personalization. For these, the index one-liner is the only published rule.
- **One broken link:** Multi-modal output links to `/patterns/observing-multimodal`, which returns the site's own "Pattern Not Found" page.

Sub-categories AIverse uses beneath phases (recovered from detail-page breadcrumbs, not published on the index): Entry touchpoints, Setting expectations, Expressive input, Expanding context, Processing, Types of outputs, Explainability, Correction & iteration, Managing memory, Collecting feedback.
