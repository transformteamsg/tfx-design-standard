New here? Start on this page. It explains what the standard is, what the plugin
and its skills do, how the design loop works, and what your team decides before
adopting. You do not need to be technical to follow it. Once you know how far you
want to hand work over, the [designer on-ramp](/harness/on-ramp) picks up where
this page ends.

## What this is

This standard has two layers, and your product can adopt both. The
**foundation** is a catalog of controls and a set of principles that every
product in the portfolio shares — how colour, type, spacing, and copy behave.
On top sits your **domain**: the part of the portfolio your product belongs to,
which sets the brand basics for products like yours. Your product inherits both,
then declares only the few things that make it its own.

<FoundationProfile />

You never restate the shared rules. You declare your values (your primary
colour, your fonts) and the standard fills in the rest from your domain, then
the foundation.

## What the plugin and skills are

The **plugin** is a package you install into Claude Code once. Claude Code is the
tool your builder already uses to write and change code; the plugin adds this
standard's know-how to it. Installing takes two lines, and you do it a single
time per repository.

A **skill** is a command you type to Claude Code. Each is named for what it does:

- `/dxd:start` orients you and points you to the right next step.
- `/dxd:setup` onboards your product by asking you a few questions (more on that
  below).
- `/dxd:design` runs the design loop to build or change a screen.

One word you will meet: an **agent** is Claude doing a task for you, step by step,
the way a capable teammate would. The skills tell the agent to follow this
standard while it works.

## How the loop works

When you ask for a screen, the `/dxd:design` skill runs a loop of six phases. The
important one is the **plan**: the agent writes down what it intends to build, and
nothing gets built until you approve it.

<OrbitLoop variant="inline" />

So you are never handed a surprise. You say what you mean, you see the plan, you
approve it, and only then does the agent build. Afterwards a separate agent checks
the result against what you asked for.

## What adopting takes

Adopting is lighter than it sounds. You need:

- a Claude Code session (your builder has one),
- about 15 minutes with the setup wizard, and
- your brand basics: your primary colour and your fonts. Your domain and
  everything else can use the standard's default.

To install the plugin, your builder runs these two lines in Claude Code, once per
repository:

```
/plugin marketplace add transformteamsg/tfx-design-standard
/plugin install dxd@dxd
```

Then `/dxd:setup` walks you through the questions above.

## How to customise

Customising means answering the wizard. It asks, one question at a time, for your
**product name**, **domain**, **audiences**, **primary colour**, **typefaces**,
**stack**, **illustration** direction, and **voice**. Every question except the
name is skippable, and skipping means "use the standard's default". You declare
values like a colour or a font name, and the standard never makes you restate a
rule.

When you finish, the wizard writes your product's `DESIGN.md` for you. You never
edit a file yourself. Want to see what your domain already sets, or change it
later? Read your domain's page under [Domains](/domains) and re-run the wizard any
time.

## Where to get help

- **Stuck or confused by the harness itself?** Say so in your session — the
  feedback skill files it for the team, even mid-task.
- **A question about the standard?** Ask the foundation owner, who maintains the
  catalog and principles.
- **What applies to a product like yours?** Your domain's page under
  [Domains](/domains) lists the brand basics and any domain-specific rules.
