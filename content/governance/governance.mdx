- **Living document.** If something feels off or outdated, speak up, or open a PR. Anyone in TransformX can propose a control, guideline, or tool. The bar for L0/L1 is high; the bar for L2 is evidence.
- **The ratchet.** The catalog grows only from observed failures, never speculation. Every escaped defect becomes a control proposal.
- **Decision records.** Significant decisions record options considered, the tradeoff named, waivers granted.
- **Waiver registry.** L1 waivers in one queryable place with approver and reason. Recurring waivers mean: fix the standard or fix the system.
- **Evidence base.** Teacher feedback as tagged, queryable evidence records; open pattern questions in one register with live status.
- **System time is not overhead.** A deliberate share of design capacity goes to improving the system (controls, checks, docs). An hour on a check script saves ten hours of review; only system work compounds.

## Domains

As the standard scales past Teacher & School, it scales as a set of domains — each
a portfolio-level brand boundary with its own products, colours, and voice. Two
roles own this:

- **Foundation owner** (a design lead) owns the foundation catalog and approves
  every ratchet today.
- **Domain lead** owns their domain's profile (`standards/domains/<slug>.yaml`)
  and site page, and proposes domain-scoped controls and profile changes.

The rules that keep this safe live in `harness/standards/README.md` under
"Domains" — this page cites them rather than restating them: a domain may only
add scoped controls, never weaken, override, or globally waive a foundation
control, and a profile carries brand parameters, never catalog rules.

A domain starts life as a stub marked `proposed`. Its domain lead declares the
profile through the ratchet; once approved, the domain is `settled` and the
lead can propose domain-scoped controls the same way any control is proposed.

**Looking ahead:** once two or more domains are actively ratcheting, a
cross-domain design council will take over approving foundation ratchets. Until
that condition is met, the foundation owner decides alone.
