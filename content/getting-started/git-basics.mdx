Git is the tool teams use to work on the same code without overwriting each other's work.

**Your computer vs the shared server.** Your own computer holds your private copy. Anything you do there (including a live preview at `localhost`) is yours alone until you push it up to the shared server. The shared server (usually GitLab, sometimes GitHub) is what the whole team sees. Keep the two apart in your head and most of what follows makes sense.

## The words you'll hear

<Glossary>

<Term icon="folder-git-2">**Repo** - the shared folder of code your team works in, hosted on GitLab or GitHub.</Term>

<Term icon="copy">**Clone** - make a copy of the repo on your own computer.</Term>

<Term icon="git-branch">**Branch** - your own private version of the code, so nothing you do touches the shared version until it's reviewed.</Term>

<Term icon="git-commit-horizontal">**Commit** - save a checkpoint of your work. Commit often; it's what lets you undo mistakes.</Term>

<Term icon="arrow-up-from-line">**Push** - upload your branch to the shared server so others can see it. Not merged yet, just uploaded.</Term>

<Term icon="git-pull-request">**Merge request** (MR; **pull request** or PR on GitHub) - ask for your branch to be reviewed and added into the shared code.</Term>

<Term icon="git-merge">**Merge** - approved and added in. Your work is now part of the shared code.</Term>

</Glossary>

That's the whole vocabulary. Your AI can run all of these for you; knowing what they mean just lets you tell when something looks off.
