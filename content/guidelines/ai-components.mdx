A component is a reusable part of an AI feature.
Most parts below come from [AI Elements](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/), a library you copy into your codebase and then own and edit.
A few are patterns with no component of their own, built from shadcn primitives such as Badge or Alert.

Pick your feature type on [Patterns](/guidelines/ai-patterns) first, then look up each component here for the "when to use" and "when not to use" call.

Components are grouped by what they do in the interface.
**Input** is what someone gives the AI.
**Output** is what the AI produces on screen.
**Trust** is what the AI shows about how it got there.
**Control** is how a person stays in charge.
**State** is how the interface shows what is happening or has gone wrong.
**Identity** is how the AI is framed as a tool, not a person.

## Composing a grounded answer for a learner

One worked example, to show how to read this page.
Say a learner asks a question about their own submitted work.
That is the [Answering from your own records](/guidelines/ai-patterns#answering-from-your-own-records) pattern.
Walking the six groups picks the parts.

- **Input.** A `Prompt input` for the question, plus `Attachments` if the work is not already on file.
- **Output.** A `Message` carrying the reply.
- **Trust.** An `AI label` on the reply, `Inline citation` on each sentence, `Sources` collapsed underneath. `Confidence` in plain words if reliability genuinely varies.
- **Control.** `Clarify` one question back if intent is unclear. No `Confirmation`, since nothing is being changed.
- **State.** `Loader` while it retrieves, `Error` if the records are unreachable.
- **Identity.** No `Persona`. In a learning setting the AI should read as a tool, not a tutor.

Every other pattern composes the same way: pick from each group, and let the pattern's rules on the Patterns page decide what to include.

## Input

The ways someone gives the AI something to work on.

### Prompt input

*A text box with tools, attachments, and a send control that doubles as stop.*

**When to use:** the request is genuinely open and you cannot list the options in advance.
**When not to use:** the task has a known shape. A form with three fields beats a blank box someone must guess at.

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/prompt-input)

### Attachments

*A strip of files the person has added to the request.*

**When to use:** the answer depends on a document only that person holds.
**When not to use:** the record already lives in your product. Reference it rather than asking for a re-upload.

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/attachments)

### Suggestion

*Tappable chips offering a prompt or a next step.*

<DoDont
  doLabel="When to use"
  dontLabel="When not to use"
  items={[
    { kind: "do", text: "You can name two or three moves that genuinely help this person right now." },
    { kind: "do", text: "The chips sit in a chat empty state or beside a form field where they belong." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "You cannot name useful moves. Generic chips like 'Tell me more' train people to skip the row." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "You want to offer five or more. That is a menu, not a suggestion." },
  ]}
/>

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/suggestion)

### Speech input

*A record button that turns speech into text.*

**When to use:** hands are busy or typing is slow.
**When not to use:** the setting is shared. Saying a learner's name aloud in a staffroom is a disclosure.

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/speech-input)

### Mic selector

*A picker for which microphone to record from.*

**When to use:** a recording has failed and the wrong input device is the likely cause.
**When not to use:** it is the first run. Offer it after a failure, not as a setting nobody asked for.

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/mic-selector)

## Output

The ways the AI shows what it produced.

### Message

*One turn from a person or the AI, with its own toolbar.*

**When to use:** the exchange is genuinely back and forth, inside a `Conversation`.
**When not to use:** it is a one-shot draft inside a form. A chat bubble implies a conversation that is not there.

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/message)

### Code block

*Syntax-highlighted source code with a copy button.*

**When to use:** the output is code, config, or a snippet meant to be copied and run somewhere else.
**When not to use:** the output is prose. A code block on plain answers pushes non-technical readers away.

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/code-block)

### Image

*An AI-generated image rendered inline.*

**When to use:** the image is decorative and nobody will read it as evidence.
**When not to use:** accuracy matters. Generated images invent text, faces, counts, and diagram labels.

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/image)

### Transcription

*Recorded speech shown as text, split into segments you can jump to.*

**When to use:** someone needs to scan or navigate a recording.
**When not to use:** the transcript will stand as the record. Names, dialect, and subject vocabulary transcribe badly.

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/transcription)

## Trust

The ways the AI shows what it did and what it drew from.

### AI label

*A badge that marks AI-produced content and explains what produced it.*

**When to use:** any AI output a person could mistake for their own work or a colleague's.
**When not to use:** the person has edited the output. Once someone rewrites the text it is theirs.

[View on shadcn ↗](https://ui.shadcn.com/docs/components/badge)

### Confidence

*A plain-language band on a result: high, medium, or low.*

<DoDont
  doLabel="When to use"
  dontLabel="When not to use"
  items={[
    { kind: "do", text: "Reliability genuinely varies from one answer to the next." },
    { kind: "do", text: "Low confidence unlocks a different action, such as routing to a person for review." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "Reliability does not vary. A badge that says the same thing every time is noise." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "There is no different action at low confidence. A badge you cannot act on shifts blame to the reader." },
  ]}
/>

[View on shadcn ↗](https://ui.shadcn.com/docs/components/badge)

### Reasoning

*A collapsible panel of the model narrating its own thinking, self-collapsing once the answer arrives.*

**When to use:** someone wants a sense of how an answer formed, and the panel disappears once the answer lands.
**When not to use:** you need evidence for a decision about a person. The trace is narration, not a log.

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/reasoning)

### Chain of thought

*A collapsible reasoning trail with a connector rail between steps.*

**When to use:** an agent's working is worth showing as narration; a collapsed default keeps the surface calm.
**When not to use:** you need proof of a decision. Use [Task](#task) instead when steps must be inspectable.

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/chain-of-thought)

### Sources

*A collapsible list of what an answer was grounded in.*

**When to use:** the answer came from a bounded set the person can open and check.
**When not to use:** the list was assembled afterwards. Sources that did not produce the answer borrow credibility they have not earned.

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/sources)

### Inline citation

*A citation marker sitting inside the sentence it supports.*

**When to use:** different sentences come from different places and the reader needs to tell which is which.
**When not to use:** one continuous claim comes from one source. Use [Sources](#sources) collapsed below the message instead.

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/inline-citation)

### Tool

*A collapsible record of one tool call, where a tool is a function the model calls to reach outside itself.*

**When to use:** the call touched a real record or the outside world, so it doubles as evidence.
**When not to use:** it is an internal call with no consequence. A wall of expanded tool calls is noise.

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/tool)

## Control

The ways a person stays in charge before, during, and after an action.

### Clarify

*One question back from the AI before it acts.*

**When to use:** the request has two plausible readings and guessing wrong would waste the person's work.
**When not to use:** you can infer the answer from context you already hold. A chain of clarifiers is a form in disguise.

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/suggestion)

### Confirmation

*An approve-or-deny gate that names exactly what will happen.*

<DoDont
  doLabel="When to use"
  dontLabel="When not to use"
  items={[
    { kind: "do", text: "Something sends, files, or changes a record about a person." },
    { kind: "do", text: "The same gate can sit inside a chat reply or on a standalone approval queue." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "The action is small and reversible." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "Every step gets a gate. That trains people to wave through the one that matters." },
  ]}
/>

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/confirmation)

### Plan

*The ordered steps an agent intends to run, before it runs them.*

**When to use:** the steps are genuinely under review before anything happens.
**When not to use:** the steps cannot be edited or refused. Use [Task](#task) for what is already running.

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/plan)

### Task

*One step of work, with its own state and any files it touched.*

**When to use:** the work runs in visible steps and someone may need to see which one failed.
**When not to use:** there is only one step. A single row with a tick is ceremony.

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/task)

### Checkpoint

*A named point in a long run that someone can return to.*

**When to use:** the run is long or branching, and a wrong turn would cost real work.
**When not to use:** the flow is short, or you cannot actually restore the state you offer.

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/checkpoint)

### Queue

*The list of requests waiting behind the one running now.*

**When to use:** someone can legitimately stack work, such as batching letters.
**When not to use:** you could remove the queue instead. A visible backlog draws attention to a wait you have not fixed.

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/queue)

## State

The ways the interface shows what is happening or what has gone wrong.

### Loader

*A small spinner for a short wait.*

**When to use:** the wait is a second or two and nothing useful can be shown yet.
**When not to use:** the wait is long enough to walk away from. Show progress or partial output with [Shimmer](#shimmer) and [Task](#task).

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/loader)

### Shimmer

*A moving highlight over text while it streams.*

**When to use:** text is actively arriving and you want the eye to follow the leading edge.
**When not to use:** nothing is streaming. A shimmer with no text arriving reads as a stall.

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/shimmer)

### Empty state

*The opening panel of a chat, before anyone has typed.*

**When to use:** the surface is open-ended and a first-time user would not know what to ask.
**When not to use:** the surface does one job and could do it directly. "Ask me anything" adds a step to something that should already run.

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/conversation)

### Error

*A failure notice that says what happened and what to do next.*

**When to use:** the AI failed and there is a real next move, even if that move is to do the task by hand.
**When not to use:** retrying cannot work. Do not offer "Try again" for a refusal or a missing permission.

[View on shadcn ↗](https://ui.shadcn.com/docs/components/alert)

### Feedback

*Thumbs up and down on a response, with an acknowledgement.*

<DoDont
  doLabel="When to use"
  dontLabel="When not to use"
  items={[
    { kind: "do", text: "The rating routes somewhere and the person can see it landed." },
    { kind: "do", text: "You will act on the aggregate." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "Nothing changes when a rating comes in." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "You collect ratings you never look at. That is free labour disguised as UX." },
  ]}
/>

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/message)

## Identity

The ways the AI is framed as a tool, not a person.

### Persona

*An animated character with idle, listening, thinking, and speaking states.*

<DoDont
  doLabel="When to use"
  dontLabel="When not to use"
  items={[
    { kind: "do", text: "A voice surface needs a visible state, and the character claims no expertise." },
    { kind: "do", text: "The setting is playful and low-stakes; nobody could mistake the character for authority." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "Any reader might read the character as authority." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "The setting is learning or assessment. A face invites trust the system has not earned." },
  ]}
/>

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/persona)

### Voice selector

*A picker for the synthesised voice that reads answers aloud.*

**When to use:** more than one voice is approved, and accent or pace is an accessibility need.
**When not to use:** only one voice is approved. Never let a chosen voice imply expertise the system does not have.

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/voice-selector)

### Model selector

*A picker for which underlying model answers.*

**When to use:** the person has a real basis to choose, such as trading speed against depth.
**When not to use:** they have no basis. Naming models makes the choice their responsibility and the outcome their fault.

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/model-selector)

### Open in chat

*A menu that hands the current query to an outside chat product.*

**When to use:** the content is public and someone wants to keep exploring elsewhere.
**When not to use:** the query could carry personal or record data. This sends that text to a third party.

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/open-in-chat)

### Context

*A hover-card readout of the current conversation's size in tokens.*

**When to use:** you are building for developers or operators who need to see how full the context window is.
**When not to use:** the reader does not manage cost or context. To a student or parent, a token count reads as noise.

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/context)

### Conversation

*The scrolling thread that holds a back-and-forth of messages.*

**When to use:** the exchange genuinely spans several turns and follow-ups are the point.
**When not to use:** the task is known. A conversation panel makes someone compose a request your form could have asked for.

[View on AI Elements ↗](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/conversation)

## Memory: nothing here yet

The one thing this standard does not ship components for is remembering across sessions.
`Checkpoint` restores a point inside one run and `Context` reports the current conversation, but both end when the session does.
If you need cross-session memory, treat it as new work.
Design what gets stored, show the person that record, and let them edit and delete it.

For the patterns this standard deliberately does not ship, see the `ai` skill's reference notes.
