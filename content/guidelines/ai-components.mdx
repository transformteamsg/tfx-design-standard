**Start here once you know your pattern and need the parts to build it.**
A component is one interface part: a prompt input, an AI label, a confidence badge, a source list.
Every part on this page tells you when to reach for it and, more importantly, when not to.
If you haven't picked a pattern yet, go to [Patterns](/guidelines/ai-patterns) first.

<GroupGrid />

Most parts come from [AI Elements](https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/), a library you copy into your codebase and then own and edit. A few are patterns built from shadcn primitives.

## Composing a grounded answer for a learner

One worked example, to show how to read this page.
Say a learner asks a question about their own submitted work.
That is the [Answering from your own records](/guidelines/ai-patterns#answering-from-your-own-records) pattern.
Walking the six groups picks the parts.

- **Input.** A `Prompt input` for the question, plus `Attachments` if the work is not already on file.
- **Output.** A `Message` carrying the reply.
- **Trust.** An `AI label` on the reply, `Inline citation` on each sentence, `Sources` collapsed underneath. `Confidence` in plain words if reliability genuinely varies.
- **Control.** `Clarify` one question back if intent is unclear. No `Confirmation`, since nothing is being changed.
- **State.** `Loader` while it retrieves, `Error` if the records are unreachable.
- **Identity.** No `Persona`. In a learning setting the AI should read as a tool, not a tutor.

Every other pattern composes the same way: pick from each group, and let the pattern's rules on the Patterns page decide what to include.

## Input

The ways someone gives the AI something to work on.

### Prompt input

*Users see a chat-style text box for typing what they want. Reach for it when the request is open-ended and no form would fit.*

<DoDont
  doLabel="When to use"
  dontLabel="When not to use"
  items={[
    { kind: "do", text: "The request is genuinely open and you cannot list the options in advance." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "The task has a known shape. A form with three fields beats a blank box someone must guess at." },
  ]}
/>

### Attachments

*Users see a strip of files they have added to the request. Reach for it when the answer depends on a document only that person holds.*

<DoDont
  doLabel="When to use"

  ]}
/>

<SourceLink href="https://elements.ai-sdk.dev/components/attachments" />

### Suggestion

*Users see tappable chips proposing what to do next. Reach for it when you can name two or three useful moves right now.*

<DoDont
  doLabel="When to use"
  dontLabel="When not to use"
  items={[

  doLabel="When to use"

  items={[
    { kind: "do", text: "Any AI output a person could mistake for their own work or a colleague's." },

<SourceLink href="https://ui.shadcn.com/docs/components/badge" label="shadcn Badge" />

### Confidence

*Users see a badge showing how sure the AI is: high, medium, or low. Reach for it when reliability varies and low confidence should route to a person.*

<DoDont
  doLabel="When to use"
  dontLabel="When not to use"

/>

*Users see an approve-or-deny gate naming exactly what will happen. Reach for it whenever the AI is about to send, file, or change a record.*

## Memory: nothing here yet

The one thing this standard does not ship components for is remembering across sessions.
`Checkpoint` restores a point inside one run and `Context` reports the current conversation, but both end when the session does.
If you need cross-session memory, treat it as new work.
Design what gets stored, show the person that record, and let them edit and delete it.

For the patterns this standard deliberately does not ship, see the `ai` skill's reference notes.
