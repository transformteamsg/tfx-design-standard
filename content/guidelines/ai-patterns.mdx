AI does a handful of distinct jobs in a product, and each has its own rules for how it should behave.
Find the job you are building below, follow its rules, and the demos show it working.
Once you know the shape, [Components](/guidelines/ai-components) has the parts you assemble it from.

## Suggestions and next steps

The AI proposes one small next action: autocomplete, a next-step chip, or a field pre-filled from context already on screen.
Use it when someone is mid-task and a nudge helps more than a new surface.
The same pattern serves an administrator picking a category, a parent completing a form, or a teacher starting a note.

- Fit the suggestion inside the existing flow. No modal, no panel.
- Make it dismissible. Nobody should feel forced to take it.
- Remove it once the person acts or types past it.
- Suggest one thing. A list of five options is a menu, not a nudge.

## Generating content

The AI writes a first draft that the person owns and then edits.
That might be a case note, a plain-language version of a policy, a reply to a parent, or a revision plan.
The person edits and submits, and the AI never finalises on their behalf.
Check first what the draft is for: drafting an administrative artefact saves real time, but drafting a learner's own writing removes the work.

- **Mark AI content where it appears,** and remove the label once the person edits the field.
- **Say how sure it is in plain words** - high, medium, or low - never as a percentage.
- **Stream the draft:** show it appearing progressively, token by token (the small text fragments the model emits), with a stop control visible.
- **On a learning task, draft the scaffold and not the piece:** an outline, a question, or a worked example on other material.

## Summarising and transforming content

The AI shortens or reshapes long material.
A year of records becomes a paragraph, a policy document becomes a plain-language notice, meeting notes become an update.
Use it when someone needs orientation or a different format, not the full source text.

- Keep the summary beside the source so any claim can be checked without navigating away.
- Every factual statement must trace back to a source passage. Leave out what cannot be traced.
- Say what the summary covers and what it excludes - the date range, the record types, the documents read.

## Answering questions from your data

Someone asks a question about a bounded set of records or documents and gets a cited answer.
Engineers call this RAG, or retrieval-augmented generation - answering from a named set of sources rather than the model's general knowledge.
The set might be a submitted piece of writing, a school's policy library, or one applicant's file.

- **Cite the passage the answer drew from,** and let the citation open the source.
- **Ask a clarifying question when intent or scope is unclear,** rather than proceeding on an assumption.
- **Say when the answer is not there:** "I cannot find this in these records" beats a confident invention.
- **Sort by question type:** facts get a direct answer, the person's own thinking gets a guiding question. Impact Bench, which tests models rather than interfaces, treats a direct answer as a failure on learning tasks.

## Classifying and flagging

The AI labels, scores, or flags records: a support tier, an application that looks incomplete, a learner who may need a check-in.
The person a label describes is usually not the person reading it, so design for both.

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Mark what the AI classified and where | Present a flag with no attribution |
| Show confidence in plain words | Show a raw probability score |
| Let the person the label describes see and contest it | Keep the label visible only to whoever operates the tool |
| Acknowledge a correction and show what it changed | Collect a correction that changes nothing |

## Agents that take action

The AI completes a whole task on someone's behalf: filing a referral, updating a record, booking a room, sending a notification.
An agent is AI that runs a sequence of steps to finish a task, rather than only producing text.
No pattern on this page carries more risk.

- Show the full plan before any step runs. Let the person remove or reorder steps.
- Require an explicit yes before anything that sends, files, or changes a record.
- Name the record, the field, and the proposed value. Never a bare "Confirm?".
- During execution, show which step is running, and pause at anything unexpected.
- Expose the agent's working as a collapsible chain, labelled as working rather than as proof.

## Conversations

Open-ended chat is the last resort, for tasks that genuinely cannot be scoped in advance.
When you already know the job, an embedded control beats a chat panel.

- Bound the scope out loud. Say what the assistant can see and what it cannot, before someone asks outside it.
- Give the first move. An empty box asks the person to guess what the assistant is for.
- Error copy says what did not work and what to do next. No model names or technical terms in user-visible text.
