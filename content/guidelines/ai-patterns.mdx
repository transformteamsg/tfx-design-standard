The rules here are distilled from these and adapted to TFX products.

This page is for designers who have picked a use case from the [Overview](/guidelines/ai) and want to know how to build it.

## Suggestions and next steps

The AI proposes a small next action - think autocomplete, a next-step chip, or a field pre-filled from existing context.
Use this when the teacher is mid-task and a single nudge would help without breaking their flow.

- Fit the suggestion inside the existing flow - no modal, no panel.
- Make it dismissible; the teacher should never feel forced.
- Remove it once the teacher acts or types past it.

## Generating content

The AI writes a first draft the teacher owns - a case note, end-of-term comment, or goal statement.
The teacher edits and submits; the AI never finalises on their behalf.

- Mark AI content where it appears. Remove the label once the teacher edits the field.
- Show certainty in plain words - high, medium, or low - not as a percentage.
- Stream from the first token, with a visible stop control throughout.

## Summarising and transforming content

The AI distils or reshapes long material - a term of records into one paragraph, or notes into a parent update.
Use this when the teacher needs orientation or a new format, not the full source text.

- Keep the summary beside the source so the teacher can check any claim without navigating away.
- Every factual statement must be traceable to its source passage. Leave out what cannot be sourced.

## Answering questions from your data

The teacher asks about a bounded set of records or documents and gets a cited answer.
Examples: student records, a submitted piece of writing, or an uploaded assessment.
[Google PAIR](https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook/patterns) calls this "grounded Q&A."

- Cite the passage the answer drew from.
- Ask the teacher to clarify when intent or scope is unclear; do not guess and proceed.

## Classifying and flagging

The AI labels, scores, or flags records - reading bands, students who may need a check-in, submissions that look incomplete.

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Mark what the AI classified and where | Present a flag without attribution |
| Show confidence in plain words | Use raw probability scores |
| Acknowledge corrections at the point they are given | Ignore feedback and re-flag the same record |

## Agents that take action

The AI completes a whole task on the teacher's behalf - filing a referral, updating a record, scheduling, or sending a notification.
The highest-stakes use case. [Microsoft HAX](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/library/) requires explicit consent before any consequential action.

- Show the full plan before any step runs. Let the teacher remove or reorder steps.
- Require an explicit yes before anything that sends, files, or changes a record. Name the record, field, and proposed value - never a bare "Confirm?".
- During execution, show which step is running and pause at anything unexpected.
- Expose the agent's working as a collapsible chain, labelled as working - not a guarantee.

## Conversations

Open-ended chat is the last resort when a task genuinely cannot be scoped in advance.
An embedded, task-specific control beats a chat panel when the job is known ahead of time.

- Bound the scope. Tell the teacher what the assistant can see and what it cannot, before they ask something outside that scope.
- Error copy says what did not work and what to do next. No model or technical terminology in user-visible text.
