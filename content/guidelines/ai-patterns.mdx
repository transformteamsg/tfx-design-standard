Seven kinds of AI feature. Pick the one that matches your user's need, follow its rules, build it from the components it names.

<PatternGrid />

## Suggesting a next step

*A suggestion helps someone keep moving without breaking their flow.*

<DoDont
  doLabel="When to use"
  dontLabel="When not to use"
  items={[
    { kind: "do", text: "Someone is mid-task and a nudge helps more than a new surface." },
    { kind: "do", text: "You can name two or three next moves that are genuinely useful right now." },
    { kind: "do", text: "The suggestion sits inside a form, an editor, or a workflow the person is already in." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "The surface has no known next step. Use [Exploring an open-ended problem](#exploring-an-open-ended-problem) instead." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "You want to offer five or more options. That is a menu, not a suggestion." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "The task is drafting something a person will own and edit. Use [Generating a first draft](#generating-a-first-draft)." },
  ]}
/>

**How it works:**

<Checklist>
  <Check>**Fit it into the existing flow.** No modal, no panel that pulls the person out of what they were doing.</Check>
  <Check>**Make it dismissible.** Nobody should feel forced to take it.</Check>
  <Check>**Clear it once the person acts.** Remove the suggestion the moment they accept, edit past it, or ignore it.</Check>
  <Check>**Offer one thing, not five.** A long list of options is a menu, and menus belong on a different surface.</Check>
</Checklist>

<ComponentsUsed items={["Suggestion", "Prompt input"]} />

**Watch out for:**
A suggestion that reappears the moment it is dismissed. Nobody feels in control.

<DemoInlineSuggest title="A suggestion right inside the field" blurb="No panel, no thread. The AI sits next to the input; accept, edit, or reject in place." />

<DemoPromptInput title="A nudge inside a chat flow" blurb="Two or three named next steps offered where the person already is, that vanish once they type." />

## Generating a first draft

*A first draft helps someone start from a written version they can edit rather than a blank page.*

<DoDont
  doLabel="When to use"
  dontLabel="When not to use"
  items={[
    { kind: "do", text: "The person owns, edits, and submits the output: a note, a reply, a plan, a policy in plain language." },
    { kind: "do", text: "The draft is admin work where writing the first version saves real time." },
    { kind: "do", text: "A person will always read the draft before it goes anywhere." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "The task is the person's own creative or learning work. Use [Suggesting a next step](#suggesting-a-next-step) with a prompt or a worked example on other material." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "The output is a label on a record. Use [Classifying a record](#classifying-a-record)." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "Every claim must be quoted from source material. Use [Summarising long material](#summarising-long-material) or [Answering from your own records](#answering-from-your-own-records) instead." },
  ]}
/>

**How it works:**

<Checklist>
  <Check>**Mark AI content where it appears.** Remove the label the moment the person edits the field.</Check>
  <Check>**Say how sure the AI is in plain words.** High, medium, or low. Never as a percentage.</Check>
  <Check>**Stream the draft with a stop control.** Text appears progressively, and a stop button is visible from the first character.</Check>
  <Check>**On a learning task, scaffold rather than write.** Draft an outline, a question, or a worked example on other material, not the piece the learner should write.</Check>
</Checklist>

<ComponentsUsed items={["Message", "Shimmer", "AI label"]} />

**Watch out for:**
A learner's essay written for them in their voice. The teaching disappears.

<DemoAiLabel title="A draft marked as AI" blurb="The badge flags AI text and clears the moment the person edits it, because then it is theirs." />

<DemoStreaming title="A draft that streams, with a stop" blurb="Text arrives progressively and can be halted from the first token. On a learning task it scaffolds rather than writing the piece." />

## Summarising long material

*A summary helps someone orient in long material without reading it all.*

<DoDont
  doLabel="When to use"
  dontLabel="When not to use"
  items={[
    { kind: "do", text: "Someone needs the shape of long material fast, such as a year of records into a paragraph." },
    { kind: "do", text: "The source stays reachable beside the summary so any claim can be checked." },
    { kind: "do", text: "The person will act on the shape, not on individual facts." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "Every fact must be quotable and defensible. Use [Answering from your own records](#answering-from-your-own-records) so each claim carries a citation." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "The material is short enough to read directly. Read it." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "The source cannot be shown alongside for legal or privacy reasons. A summary without its source cannot be checked." },
  ]}
/>

**How it works:**

<Checklist>
  <Check>**Keep the summary beside the source.** Any claim should be checkable without navigating away.</Check>
  <Check>**Trace every factual statement to a source passage.** Leave out what cannot be traced.</Check>
  <Check>**Say what the summary covers and what it excludes.** Name the date range, the record types, the documents read.</Check>
</Checklist>

<ComponentsUsed items={["Message", "Sources", "Inline citation"]} />

**Watch out for:**
A summary that reads clean because it dropped the caveats and hedges from the source.

<DemoSources title="A summary you can trace" blurb="Every claim opens the source passage it came from, so nothing has to be taken on trust." />

## Answering from your own records

*A grounded answer helps someone get a specific answer from records they hold, with the source in reach.*

Engineers call this **RAG**, or retrieval-augmented generation: answering from a named set of sources rather than the model's general knowledge.
The set might be a submitted piece of writing, a school's policy library, or one applicant's file.

<DoDont
  doLabel="When to use"
  dontLabel="When not to use"
  items={[
    { kind: "do", text: "The question is specific and the answer lives in a bounded set of records or documents." },
    { kind: "do", text: "The person can open the cited passage and check it for themselves." },
    { kind: "do", text: "The record set stays under your product's control, not the model's." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "The question is fuzzy or open-ended. Use [Exploring an open-ended problem](#exploring-an-open-ended-problem)." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "The answer is the person's own thinking on a learning task. Use [Suggesting a next step](#suggesting-a-next-step) and guide, don't answer." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "The record set is unbounded or public web content. Grounding cannot help here." },
  ]}
/>

**How it works:**

<Checklist>
  <Check>**Cite the passage each claim came from.** Let the citation open the source in one tap.</Check>
  <Check>**Ask one clarifying question when intent is unclear.** Better than proceeding on the wrong reading.</Check>
  <Check>**Say when the answer is not there.** "I cannot find this in these records" beats a confident invention.</Check>
  <Check>**Sort the reply by task type.** Facts get a direct answer; the person's own thinking gets a guiding question.</Check>
</Checklist>

<ComponentsUsed items={["Message", "Inline citation", "Sources", "Clarify"]} />

**Watch out for:**
A confident answer when the answer is not in the records.

<DemoInlineCitation title="An answer with inline citations" blurb="Each sentence carries the source it drew from, so the reader can check the claim in place." />

<DemoClarify title="One question back before answering" blurb="When the request has two readings, the AI asks which one rather than guessing and wasting the work." />

## Classifying a record

*A classification helps someone triage many records by adding a label, score, or flag to each.*

The person the label describes is usually not the person reading it, so design for both.

<DoDont
  doLabel="When to use"
  dontLabel="When not to use"
  items={[
    { kind: "do", text: "The record set is larger than one person can read, and a label helps them triage." },
    { kind: "do", text: "The person the label describes can see it and contest it in place." },
    { kind: "do", text: "Low confidence unlocks a different action, such as routing to a person for review." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "A fixed rule can set the same label. Use the rule and skip the AI." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "The label decides a consequential outcome the person cannot appeal. Do not build this." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "The classifier's reasons cannot be shown. A label a person cannot understand is one they cannot contest." },
  ]}
/>

**How it works:**

<Checklist>
  <Check>**Mark what the AI classified and where.** Never present a flag with no attribution.</Check>
  <Check>**Show confidence in plain words.** High, medium, or low. Never a raw score.</Check>
  <Check>**Let the person the label describes see it.** They should be able to read the reason and correct it in place.</Check>
  <Check>**Acknowledge a correction and show what it changed.** A correction that changes nothing is theatre.</Check>
</Checklist>

<ComponentsUsed items={["Confidence", "AI label", "Feedback"]} />

**Watch out for:**
A flag visible to the operator but not to the person the label describes.

<DemoClassifierRow title="A flagged row on a table" blurb="The person the label describes can read the reason and correct it in place, without ever entering a chat." />

<DemoConfidence title="A flag with plain-language confidence" blurb="High, medium, or low, never a raw score, and only where low confidence changes what happens next." />

<DemoFeedback title="A correction the loop acts on" blurb="Feedback that visibly lands and changes something, not a rating that collects free labour." />

## Running an agent on someone's behalf

*An agent helps someone finish a whole multi-step task without doing the steps by hand, while keeping them in control at each gate.*

An **agent** is AI that runs a sequence of steps to finish a task, rather than only producing text.
No pattern on this page carries more risk.

<DoDont
  doLabel="When to use"
  dontLabel="When not to use"
  items={[
    { kind: "do", text: "The task is repetitive, multi-step, and the person genuinely wants to delegate it." },
    { kind: "do", text: "Every step that changes state can be named up front and gated." },
    { kind: "do", text: "A person can approve, deny, or edit each step before it runs." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "Any step is unrecoverable and cannot be gated. Do not automate it." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "The task is fuzzy or cannot be planned ahead. Use [Exploring an open-ended problem](#exploring-an-open-ended-problem) or [Suggesting a next step](#suggesting-a-next-step)." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "The person cannot verify what the agent did after it ran. If you cannot show the record and field it touched, do not build this." },
  ]}
/>

**How it works:**

<Checklist>
  <Check>**Show the full plan before any step runs.** The person can remove or reorder steps.</Check>
  <Check>**Require an explicit yes before anything that sends, files, or changes a record.** No silent action.</Check>
  <Check>**Name the record, the field, and the proposed value.** Never a bare "Confirm?".</Check>
  <Check>**Show which step is running during execution.** Pause at anything unexpected.</Check>
  <Check>**Expose the agent's working as a collapsible chain.** Labelled as working, not as proof of a decision.</Check>
</Checklist>

<ComponentsUsed items={["Plan", "Confirmation", "Task", "Chain of thought"]} />

**Watch out for:**
A "Confirm?" gate that does not name the record, the field, or the value.

<DemoAgentQueue title="Agent runs as a work queue" blurb="Plans and gates on a standalone surface, not inside a chat. Approve or deny each pending run independently." />

<DemoPlan title="The steps an agent proposes" blurb="Visible, editable, refusable. A plan you cannot change is a progress bar in costume." />

<DemoConfirmation title="Approve before it acts" blurb="The exact record, field, and value named in plain words, waiting for a yes." />

<DemoTask title="Which step is running" blurb="Visible steps during execution, so a person can see where it is and which one failed." />

<DemoChainOfThought title="The working, labelled as working" blurb="A collapsible reasoning trail shown as narration, not offered as proof of a decision." />

## Exploring an open-ended problem

*Open chat helps someone work through a question whose shape cannot be scoped ahead of time.*

Chat is the fallback pattern.
If a task fits any of the six above, build that instead - an embedded control beats a chat panel every time.

<DoDont
  doLabel="When to use"
  dontLabel="When not to use"
  items={[
    { kind: "do", text: "The task is genuinely fuzzy: someone does not yet know what they are asking for." },
    { kind: "do", text: "The answer needs several turns of clarification before it lands." },
    { kind: "do", text: "You have ruled out every other pattern above." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "The task is a nudge or autocomplete. Use [Suggesting a next step](#suggesting-a-next-step)." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "The task produces a written artefact. Use [Generating a first draft](#generating-a-first-draft)." },
    { kind: "dont", text: "The task adds a label to a record. Use [Classifying a record](#classifying-a-record)." },
  ]}
/>

**How it works:**

<Checklist>
  <Check>**Bound the scope out loud.** Say what the assistant can see and what it cannot, before someone asks outside it.</Check>
  <Check>**Give the first move.** An empty box asks the person to guess what the assistant is for.</Check>
  <Check>**Write errors in plain words.** Say what did not work and what to do next. No model names or technical terms.</Check>
</Checklist>

<ComponentsUsed items={["Conversation", "Message", "Prompt input", "Error"]} />

**Watch out for:**
A chat panel opened for a task any of the six above would do better.

<DemoError title="A failure with a way forward" blurb="Says what broke and the next move, in plain words, with no model names in sight." />
